GUIDE 01

Build a bursary application that is ready to submit

A practical document checklist, funding comparison and submission routine for South African learners.

Start with eligibility, then build a shortlist

Before writing a motivational letter, read the provider’s criteria and separate compulsory requirements from preferences. Record the qualification, institution, study year, academic requirements and any location or household circumstances mentioned. If a rule is unclear, ask the provider using contact details on its official website. An attractive award is not useful if your programme is excluded. Make a shortlist of opportunities that fit your situation and keep a separate record for options that need clarification. This prevents you from spending all your available time on an application you cannot qualify for.

Prepare a document folder without oversharing

Use the application instructions as your checklist rather than a generic social media list. A provider may request identification, recent results, proof of admission and financial supporting documents, but requirements differ. Check that scans are readable, complete and within the portal’s file limits. Use clear filenames such as Surname_Results.pdf and store originals securely. Upload sensitive documents only through a verified application channel. Never publish identity numbers, bank details or full household documents in a community post. If certified copies are requested, check the provider’s rules about certification and recency before submitting.

Compare the full funding offer

Two bursaries with similar headlines may cover very different costs. Write down whether tuition, registration, books, accommodation, transport and meals are covered, capped or excluded. Read the renewal conditions and any service, work or repayment obligations in the agreement. Ask how payments are made and what happens if you change course or repeat a module. For a personal budget, subtract confirmed support from your expected costs and label any outstanding funding as uncertain. A submitted application is not an award, so keep a realistic backup plan while waiting for a decision.

GUIDE 02

Prepare for NSFAS without falling for application scams

Keep control of your account, follow official instructions and distinguish an application from confirmed funding.

Use the official starting point

Start at the NSFAS website and follow its links to the application channel. A message with a logo or a familiar-looking profile is not proof that it comes from NSFAS. The official guidance states that applying is free and warns applicants against sharing passwords or one-time passwords. Do not pay a stranger who promises approval or a faster result. If someone is helping you use a computer, enter your own credentials and sign out afterwards. Type the website address yourself when a forwarded link looks suspicious.

Prepare your information before opening the form

Read the current instructions for your application cycle and situation. Use the names and identification details shown on your official records and keep access to your own phone number and email. Check the documents the official system actually requests; do not assume a checklist from a previous year applies unchanged. Prepare readable files and keep a private backup. When household information is requested, answer accurately and ask the official support channel if a category does not fit your circumstances. Do not guess an answer just to move to the next screen.

Admission and funding require separate attention

Being admitted to a programme does not by itself mean your study costs have been funded. Likewise, submitting a funding application does not confirm a final award. Check both your institution’s admission process and the NSFAS funding process. The official NSFAS information explains that funding depends on the relevant eligibility rules and approved qualifications at eligible public universities or TVET colleges. Before committing to costs, verify your own programme and status through the appropriate official channels. Treat online estimates and community advice as starting points rather than individual funding decisions.

GUIDE 03

Compare a university or TVET course before you apply

Look beyond the course title: compare requirements, recognition, practical training and the full cost.

Work backwards from the work you want to do

Write down the kind of work that interests you and the tasks you would expect to perform. Then compare the actual modules, practical components and progression routes in each programme. Similar course titles can describe different qualifications. Ask what graduates can progress to, whether workplace experience is included and how that experience is arranged. If the career has a professional registration requirement, verify that route with the relevant professional body. A promotional claim should be checked against the institution’s official programme information before you pay an application or registration fee.

Check recognition and the exact programme

Verify the institution and the qualification rather than relying on a certificate image or a promise in an advertisement. The Department of Higher Education and Training publishes registers for checking private institutions. Match the legal institution name, campus and programme details carefully and ask questions where the information does not agree. Registration and accreditation checks can involve different bodies depending on the qualification. If you cannot establish who awards the qualification and which recognition applies, pause payment and request written clarification. Keep the documents you used to make your decision.

Build a comparison table with real costs

For each option, record duration, subject prerequisites, minimum marks, application process, campus and travel requirements. Add tuition, registration, equipment, data, accommodation and transport to your budget. Distinguish an advertised fee from the total annual cost and ask about refund rules before paying a deposit. Compare learning support, disability support, access to laboratories or workshops and realistic travel time. A course that looks cheaper on tuition alone may be harder to complete if daily transport costs or timetable clashes are high. Mark any unknown costs so you can follow them up.

GUIDE 04

Understand admission points and subject requirements

Calculate carefully, check each institution’s rules and plan alternatives without treating an estimate as an offer.

There is no single points rule for every course

Admission point scores help institutions interpret school results, but the calculation and exclusions can differ. A university may publish a general points table and then set additional rules for particular programmes. Another programme may consider individual subjects, selection tests or other evidence. Begin with the exact qualification and the current prospectus for the year you intend to enter. Record which subjects are included, how percentages convert to points and whether any subjects are treated differently. Do not apply a points table from one institution to another without checking it.

Separate overall points from subject minimums

A total above the stated threshold may still be insufficient if you do not meet a required subject mark. Create one line for each compulsory subject and compare your result with the published requirement. Pay particular attention when a programme distinguishes Mathematics from Mathematical Literacy or specifies a language or science subject. Meeting minimum requirements can still leave selection conditions outstanding. If your results are pending, note whether the institution considers the results you currently have and what final conditions apply. Ask admissions directly when the wording is unclear.

Use a worked planning example correctly

Imagine an institution’s own table gives your selected subjects points of 6, 5, 5, 4, 4 and 3. Their sum is 27. This is only an example of adding points after the applicable conversion rules have been established; it is not a national conversion table. Next check each programme-specific subject rule. A required subject below its minimum can matter even when the total is high enough. Finally record any further selection requirement. Keep the prospectus page or link beside your calculation so you can explain where every rule came from.

GUIDE 05

Turn past papers into a study plan that targets weak topics

A repeatable routine for timed practice, useful marking and checking whether your understanding has improved.

Choose a paper that matches your course

Start with your subject, grade, language and the relevant curriculum or assessment guidance. Check whether a paper uses an older format before treating its mark allocation as a guide to your next exam. The Department of Basic Education provides an official past-paper resource for National Senior Certificate revision. Use the matching memorandum and any required answer book or source material. Download the files while you have a reliable connection, name them clearly and keep the paper separate from the memorandum until you have attempted the questions.

Attempt first, then mark honestly

Choose a quiet practice period and write under a realistic time limit. Read instructions about question choice, calculators, diagrams and working. Record where you became stuck without opening the memorandum immediately. When marking, distinguish a correct method with a calculation error from a wrong method or a missing concept. Do not award yourself full credit because an answer looks familiar. Use the memorandum to understand the expected reasoning and ask a teacher about equivalent methods or marking decisions you cannot resolve. Keep the original attempt so you can compare it with later work.

Make an error log that changes your revision

For each significant error, record the question, topic, reason for losing marks and a specific next action. “Study harder” is too vague. A useful action might be to practise drawing force diagrams, explain a biological process without notes, or compare two sources before writing a history paragraph. Group repeated mistakes so you can see whether the main problem is knowledge, interpretation, method or time management. Work through the underlying concept in a textbook or with a teacher before attempting another set. Rewriting the memorandum alone may hide a gap in understanding.

GUIDE 06

Make a school-leaver plan with dates, costs and alternatives

Bring study choices, applications, document preparation and a realistic student budget into one tracker.

Use one tracker for separate processes

Create a table with a row for every institution, programme and funding application. Include the official link, required documents, closing date, reference number and next action. Keep admission, accommodation and funding as separate entries when they have different processes. A successful step in one does not automatically complete the others. Check dates on official pages and record when you checked them. Put reminders before deadlines so there is time to resolve a missing document or an account access problem. Do not wait until your final results to start researching routes that accept earlier applications.

Budget for the months before the first class

List the costs that may arise before teaching starts as well as the annual study expenses. These can include application charges where applicable, document preparation, travel, registration, equipment and an accommodation deposit. For each cost, record the amount, due date and whether support is confirmed. Do not count an unapproved bursary as money already available. Ask the institution or accommodation provider about payment and refund terms before committing. A simple cash-flow calendar helps you notice a payment gap early enough to investigate options through official support channels.

Choose an alternative you would actually use

Discuss at least one alternative route with a teacher, careers adviser or admissions office. Compare its entry requirements, qualification outcome, total cost and possible progression. Avoid choosing an option only because it sounds like an easy transfer into a different programme; progression needs to be verified. If you consider work or a gap period, identify a specific goal such as improving a subject result, gaining relevant experience or saving toward a known cost. Give that goal a timetable and a review date so the plan has practical next steps.

Use official application channels. Do not post identity documents or passwords in a public feed. Check the provider’s requirements, the full cost and the current deadline before you apply. A listing, admission-points estimate or submitted application does not guarantee admission or funding.
